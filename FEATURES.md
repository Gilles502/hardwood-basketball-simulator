# What is in Hardwood Basketball Simulator

A tour of everything in the game today (v0.1 Early Access). For install instructions see the [README](README.md).

Hardwood is a basketball front-office simulator. You take over one franchise in a fictional 30-team league that
starts in 2002 and run it for as many seasons as you like. The 29 other teams are not scripts: each one is run by a
CPU general manager with a name, a philosophy, skills, a plan and an owner who judges him. Everything they do comes
with a reason you can read.

## Your job

- **Pick your role** when you start a career: GM and coach, GM only, coach only, or spectator (watch the league run
  itself).
- **League settings:** salary cap rules (realistic, soft cap, no cap), trade difficulty (easy, realistic, hardcore),
  injury frequency, play-in tournament, scouting fog of war, whether the owner can fire you.
- **The owner and the board.** Your owner has a personality (big spender, demanding, balanced, rebuilder, penny
  pincher) that sets how patient they are and how much they will spend. Each season comes with objectives and an approval
  rating. Fail for too long and your job is at risk; do well and you earn a new contract, badges and achievements.
- **The job market.** Other franchises can offer you a job. You can move to a new team and keep your career.

## The CPU general managers

This is the heart of the game.

- **Every CPU team has a GM**: a person with an age, a career and a fixed philosophy. Philosophies include pick
  hoarder, youth first, trade happy, veteran lover, star chaser, defense first, offense first, cap guardian, loyalist
  and patient builder. Two teams with the same goal go about it in different ways.
- **Skills** in trading, contracts and the draft decide how good a GM is at his job: how much he pays, how well he
  reads a prospect, how often he gets fleeced. A low-skill GM makes mistakes; a strong one squeezes you.
- **A plan with memory.** Each team is contending, chasing the playoffs, stuck in the middle or rebuilding. The plan
  changes only at the start of the season or at the trade deadline, and always for a stated reason.
- **The GM follows his coach.** A defensive coach gets a GM looking for defenders.
- **Owners judge.** Each season the owner compares the results with what he expected. A team built to win that
  loses fires the coach first; if it keeps failing, the GM goes too. A rebuild on schedule is given time.
- **GMs come and go.** They age, improve with experience and results, retire, get fired and get hired. A GM market
  holds the free GMs, historical newcomers who arrive in later years, and new young GMs. Owners look for the
  opposite of what just failed, and the best GMs can turn down a hopeless team. A young unknown can grow into the
  best GM in the league.
- **You can read all of it** on the Front Office & Board page: every GM's card, philosophy, skills, plan and the
  reason for it, career, and a log of the moves made for the plan ("traded the veteran for two picks", "let the
  31-year-old walk", "drafted for upside").
- **Coach of the Year and GM of the Year**, every season.

## Trades

- **Every CPU trade starts from a wish.** At each trade window every GM writes down what he wants: a starter at a
  position, a star, picks, young players, to get under the tax, to dump a long contract, to rent a veteran for a title
  run, to sell an expiring player, to move up in the draft, to give an unhappy player a new start. A trade only
  happens when it serves a wish, and every team in it has its own reason.
- **Many kinds of trade:** star upgrades, prospects for a win-now veteran, positional swaps, veteran sales, deadline
  rentals, tax dumps paid with picks, expiring contracts for long ones, change-of-scenery swaps, trade demands,
  draft-night moves up and down. Players on the block go to an **auction** among the teams that want them.
- **Three- and four-team trades** when two teams cannot match salaries: a third team with room joins, but only
  because it gets something it wants.
- **The plan says no.** A rebuilding team will not take on 30-year-olds with long deals or give up its young core; a
  contender will not give away its best player; a cap-guardian GM will not cross the tax line.
- **Trade requests.** Players stuck on a losing team grow frustrated over the season. An important player who has had
  enough asks to be traded, and his request stays until he moves or things really get better. His team shops him.
  Your own players can ask out too: you are never forced to trade, but a player you keep will not re-sign. A
  league-wide list shows every request.
- **Same rules for you.** Your offers face the same acceptance, the same salary rules and the same era thresholds as
  any CPU deal. Tools: trade machine, "generate offers" finder, trade block, counter-offers, draft-day trades.
- **Every trade is explained** in the trade log, written as the GM's notebook, and graded A+ to F for each team on day
  one.

## Contracts and free agency

- **A salary cap that follows the era**: historical cap growth, luxury tax, aprons in the modern era, mid-level and
  biannual exceptions, minimum contracts, a rookie scale, a maximum contract.
- **Loyalty is earned** with years on the team and a happy locker room.
- **Stars walk away** from hopeless teams: an older star on a rebuild, or a player who asked out, will not extend
  and will not re-sign. You see it coming ("won't negotiate", and why). At the deadline his team can still trade him.
- **CPU renewals follow the plan.** A contender fights to keep its star, a rebuilder lets aging veterans go, a
  cheap owner stays under the tax.
- **Free agency in 12 days**, restricted and unrestricted. Teams that really want a player **raise their offers**
  night after night, up to what their plan and owner allow, and pull out when the price is too high. Stars get
  bidding wars; veterans chase rings for less; you watch the race in the offers panel.
- **Player and team options**, extensions during the season, cut-down day before the new season.

## The draft

- **Lottery night** with the exact odds of every team, then the draw.
- **Scouting:** your scouts reveal what they know about the class (with fog of war on, nothing is certain).
- **Generated draft classes** every year, with prospects from around the world and different backgrounds
  (generational talents, raw athletes, ready-made seniors, defensive anchors, pure scorers, playmakers, injury-prone
  bets).
- **CPU teams draft by need and by upside**: a rebuilder swings for the ceiling, a contender wants help now, every
  team weighs the holes in its roster. A GM sees a prospect's potential only as well as his draft skill allows.
- **Draft-night trades:** teams move up only for a prospect they really want, and the price depends on how good the
  class is at that slot.
- **The draft room:** the board, the picks as they happen, your picks, trades on the clock.

## Players: ratings, growth and aging

- **Ratings** for shooting from every zone, free throws, drawing fouls, passing, ball handling, rebounding,
  perimeter and interior defense, blocks, steals, post play, dunking, athleticism and basketball IQ, plus
  **tendencies** that decide how a player plays (how often he shoots threes, drives, posts up, passes, takes over).
- **Position-aware overall.** A player's rating at each position compares him with the players who really play it.
- **Growth from playing well.** Each summer young players grow toward their potential, faster when they play real
  minutes and produce. Performance is measured against the league of that year, so the modern scoring boom does not
  inflate everyone.
- **Potential is not fixed.** Young players can break out or stall, so there are steals and busts.
- **Aging.** Players decline in their thirties; stars who still play at a high level age more slowly. Retirements
  come with age and falling ratings.
- **Morale, chemistry and frustration.** Players react to their role and the team's results.
- **Injuries** with realistic lengths in days, from day-to-day knocks to season-ending injuries.

## Coaching and the game engine

- **Possession-by-possession simulation.** Every possession picks a ball handler and a play (pick and roll,
  spot-up, isolation, cuts, hand-offs, post-ups), builds an advantage, and ends in a shot, a pass, a turnover or a
  foul. Shots depend on the shooter, the defender, help defense and fatigue.
- **Full box scores** with advanced stats, shot zones, assisted baskets, clutch numbers and play-by-play.
- **14 coaching styles** (Pace & Space, Triangle Offense, Grit & Grind, Twin Towers, Small Ball, System Basketball
  and more), each with strengths and weaknesses and its own mix of plays.
- **Points of emphasis** for the game plan: pound the paint, hunt for threes, feed the hot hand, push the pace,
  protect the paint, run shooters off the line, full-court press.
- **Your tools as coach:** starting lineup, minutes, roles, playbook, and an opponent tab that shows their threats
  against your defenders.
- **Coaches** have their own ratings, careers and contracts. They improve after seasons beyond expectations, decline
  and retire. You can hire from a coach market.

## A league that changes with the era

The league you start in 2002 plays like 2002: slower, more post-ups, fewer threes, hand-checking. Year after year
the game moves toward the modern league: pace and space, three-point volume, different foul calls, fewer turnovers,
load management for stars. Minutes caps, the cap and the contract rules follow the years too. Every rule in the trade
and contract AI is read from the league being played, so it still makes sense 25 seasons later.

## The season and the offseason

- **An 82-game calendar** with opening night and holiday games, and no three games in three nights.
- **Simulate** one day at a time, day by day with a Stop button, or straight to the trade deadline, the All-Star
  break or the end of the regular season.
- **Play-in and playoffs** with a full bracket.
- **The End of Season Hub** walks you through nine steps: season recap, contract options, development and
  retirements, staff changes and the lottery, pre-draft trades, the draft, free agency, roster cuts, new season.

## League life

- **News** ranked by importance: trades, signings, injuries, firings and hirings, trade requests, rivalries,
  milestones.
- **Rivalries** grow from close games, playoff meetings and division battles, and fade with time.
- **Storylines**: the MVP race, streaks, revenge games against old teams, and a short story on every player card.
- **All-Star Weekend**: selections, rising stars, three-point contest, dunk contest and the game.
- **Awards**: MVP, Finals MVP, Defensive Player, Rookie, Sixth Man, Most Improved, All-League teams, All-Stars,
  Coach of the Year, GM of the Year.

## Numbers

- **Player database** with every player and every season of the save, full regular-season and playoff careers, a
  stat finder and progression charts.
- **League analytics**: advanced stats, rebounding, clutch, shot zones, team four factors, splits.
- **Finances and the CBA**: every team's payroll against the cap, tax and aprons, top salaries, bargains, overpays and
  each team's salary outlook.
- **History**: champions season by season, a Hall of Fame, league records, all-time leaders and each franchise's
  trophy room with banners and retired jerseys.

## Leagues, mods and saves

- **Fictional league** out of the box: teams, players, coaches, GMs and owners are invented.
- **Leagues are data files.** A league is one JSON file (teams, players, staff) checked with clear error messages;
  draft classes can be added too. Build a mod league from the Mods screen. See [mods/README.md](mods/README.md).
- **Safe saves**: a save is written all at once, with one backup of the previous state, so a crash never leaves a
  broken save.
