# Changelog

## v0.1.2 Early Access

The long-save update: four 25-30 season test careers, with and without real-name mods, and what they showed.

**New**
- Stars can ask for a trade: a frustration meter, a Requests tab on the Trades page, and an asking price that
  falls while they wait. Your own players can ask too: they go on the trade block, never forced out.
- Manual trades get real offers (packages of starters and picks), and you can choose the protection of your own
  first-round pick.
- Young players' potential can change: breakouts and setbacks every summer.
- Injuries are counted in days and heal over the summer; long and season-ending injuries happen again.
- The calendar shows results while a long simulation runs, with the best performance of each day.
- Coach and GM firings, hirings and retirements make the news.
- A faster draft: Auto runs four times quicker, and one click sims to your next pick or, once your picks are
  done, to the end of the draft (CPU trades no longer stop it: they stay on the draft wire).

**On the court**
- Players look more like themselves: passers pass, scorer-passers score more, creators who are not point guards
  run the offense. Stars can take 25 shots a game, but high volume costs them percentage.
- A lone star on a weak team no longer scores 35-40 a night: the defense crowds him at the rim, bigs included.
- Post scorers with a good jumper use the fadeaway instead of always going to the rim.
- CPU teams choose their go-to scorers again (they kept their first-season names for good).
- Stars who still play well age slowly and keep their role; veterans in their early 30s keep their touches.
- Players who improve no longer all end up wanting every ball: after 25 seasons most stars had the maximum usage.
- Rebuilding teams are recognised during the season and play their young players.
- Modern seasons have real-league turnovers, fouls and three-point percentage.

**Long saves**
- No rating inflation in the modern era; generated draft classes are as strong as the curated ones and their
  players keep their role.
- CPU trades no longer dry up after a few seasons; lottery teams' future firsts are priced correctly.
- No contract above 35% of the cap; thrifty owners stay near the luxury tax.
- Every team plays 82 games; the playoffs play only a little slower than the regular season.
- Fewer, better rivalry stories.

**Awards and history**
- Defensive Player of the Year weighs the team's record and defense, with voter fatigue; blocks count as much as
  steals, and the MVP rarely wins it too.
- A real Most Improved Player, a Rookie of the Year in the first season, a stricter Hall of Fame.
- Coaches grow only after good seasons, decline with age and retire; career records are counted correctly.
- Saves no longer store the same retired player several times.

## v0.1.1 Early Access

- Fixed: the game crashed at start when the zip was downloaded from GitHub and extracted with Explorer
  (Windows blocked a file the game window needs). The game now unblocks its own files; if it still cannot,
  it explains how to unblock the zip.

## v0.1.0 Early Access

First public release.

- A fictional 30-team league starting in 2002. Real-name leagues can be added as mods.
- CPU general managers with skills, traits and a franchise plan; their trades come from what they want and
  explain why they happened.
- Contracts: extensions, player and team options, stars who walk away from losing teams, same-night
  free-agency auctions.
- Draft: lottery, scouting, draft board, CPU picks by fit and GM skill, draft-night trades.
- Owners judge coaches and GMs; firings, hirings and retirements across the league.
- Day-by-day live simulation with Stop, or sim to the trade deadline, the All-Star break or the end of the
  regular season.
- Player database with full careers, League Analytics (advanced, rebounding, clutch, shot zones), awards,
  records, Hall of Fame and news.
- Saves are written all-or-nothing with a backup copy.
